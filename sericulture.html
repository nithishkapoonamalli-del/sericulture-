Explore the Lifecycle

Silkworm Lifecycle Stages

1. Egg (Ovum)

Female moths lay hundreds of eggs. Controlled temperature and humidity help eggs hatch uniformly into larvae.

2. Larva (Silkworm)

The feeding stage where larvae consume fresh mulberry leaves and molt four times as they grow rapidly.

3. Pupa (Cocoon)

Mature larvae spin a continuous silk filament around themselves to form a protective cocoon over several days.

4. Adult Moth

Moths emerge from unreeled breeding cocoons to mate and repeat the cycle, completing the life history.

Silk Varieties

Mulberry Silk

Produced by domesticated Bombyx mori fed on mulberry leaves; fine, uniform, and lustrous.

Tussar Silk

Harvested from wild silkworms feeding on forest trees like Arjun and Asan; known for rich texture and warmth.

Eri Silk

Spun from open-ended cocoons without harming the pupa; often called peace silk with a soft, cotton-like feel.

Muga Silk

Naturally golden-hued silk produced by Antheraea assamensis on Som and Soalu host plants.

Core Sericulture Practices

Host Plant Agronomy

Maintain soil fertility, drip irrigation, and timely pruning so tender, nutrient-rich leaves are ready for rearing.

Rearing Hygiene & Climate

Disinfect rearing trays and rooms, monitor temperature and airflow, and prevent disease outbreaks like pebrine or grasserie.

Cocoon Sorting & Reeling

Grade cocoons by quality and shell weight before stifling and reeling continuous filaments into raw silk yarn.
